# The meal framework

Everything from the conversation that produced Streakproof's food side, in one
place. This is the *reasoning* — the schema and the seeded template encode it,
but the "why" only existed in chat until now.

Written for Sean specifically, but the principles generalize; the AI builder's
job is to produce this shape for anyone.

---

## 1. The core insight

A good lifting program doesn't work because the exercises are optimal. It works
because **it removed the decision from the moment you're least equipped to make
one.** You don't stand in the gym at 6am asking what to train — that was decided
last week by a version of you with more executive function.

Food fails for exactly the opposite reason. The decision lands at 7pm when
you're hungry, depleted, and the delivery app is one thumb away.

So the entire job is moving the decision upstream, not making better decisions
in the moment. Everything below follows from that.

---

## 2. The gym plan, mapped 1:1

The original ADHD gym plan ("Gym Reboot") already had the right structure. The
food side is the same structure with different content.

| Gym | Food |
|---|---|
| A/B/C rotation — same four buckets (legs / push / pull / core), different lifts so it never goes stale | Same four buckets — **protein / base / veg / sauce** — with three or four rotating flavour profiles |
| The Pool day: low-friction alternate that still counts | The **four-minute fallback meal** — named, pre-decided, part of the plan |
| "Never miss twice" | Miss a *prep session* and you don't wait for next Sunday — you do a 20-minute store run for pre-cooked components |
| Progressive overload, 2-for-2 rule | Add **one** food upgrade at a time, and only once the current one has been automatic for two weeks |
| 6-minute warm-up (bike, leg swings) — not "work out" | The prep ritual: podcast on, oven to 425, water boiling, containers out. Kills the "where do I even start" freeze |
| "Shrink the start to shoes on" | The meal must require **zero assembly** at the moment of hunger |
| In-season / off-season toggle | Game nights (dodgeball Tue, kickball Sat) are *assigned* as unplanned meals, not failure points |
| Log your weights — beating a number is the dopamine | Log protein only. One number, not six |

## 3. The design principles

**Components, not meals.** Five identical Tupperwares of chicken and rice is the
food equivalent of "bench press, every day, forever." You'll do it Monday and by
Wednesday the sight of it is repulsive — that's not weak willpower, it's a real
and predictable response to sameness. Instead batch *components*: two proteins,
one or two bases, a couple of veg, and three or four sauces.

**The sauce carries the variety.** The base stays boring and constant so the
flavour axis can move. Same chicken and rice: Thai peanut Monday, chipotle-lime
Wednesday, garlic-soy Friday. Three different meals for eleven extra seconds of
work. Same logic as the squat staying fixed while accessory work rotates.

**Additive, never subtractive.** The framing isn't "cut out X." Protein and veg
get added *first*, then you eat what you want. In practice that's mostly the
same food you already eat with 30g more protein and a vegetable next to it. A
plan that says "never eat pizza again" is optimized for someone who doesn't need
a plan.

**A defined floor.** Every good program has a bad-day version. The real failure
mode isn't eating badly — it's *nothing prepped, no plan, order out, feel bad,
abandon the system*. So the floor meal is decided in advance: rotisserie
chicken and bagged salad, four minutes. Not a failure state, a tier of the plan.

**Visibility is not optional.** Prepped food in an opaque container behind the
milk does not exist. Clear containers, front shelf, eye level. Same reason the
gym bag lives by the door.

**Start with one slot.** You'd never start someone at six days a week. Don't
start at 21 meals. Pick the single meal that currently goes worst and solve only
that for three weeks. The rest stays as chaotic as it is now.

## 4. Sean's specific constraints

- **Cooking capacity:** two short sessions a week (~45–60 min), not one Sunday
  marathon.
- **Prep days:** Sunday, plus **Wednesday right after the gym** — stacked onto a
  habit that already exists, when he's already out and in motion. Thursday
  doesn't work (not enough time between end of work and DnD).
- **Goals:** more protein, more veg and fibre, less ultraprocessed, lower
  overall calories.
- **Kitchen is an easy walk** from where he works — so a dedicated front-of-
  fridge shelf is enough; no desk stash needed.
- **Favourite cuisines:** Asian first, Latin a close second.
- **Go-to foods:** sushi, ramen, pho, chicken tikka masala with naan, pasta,
  Bolay.
- **Weekly rhythm:** lifts Mon/Wed/Fri evenings, dodgeball Tuesday, kickball
  Saturday morning, DnD Thursday.

### He forgets to eat

The single most important constraint. First meal is often around midday — two
breakfast burritos at noon is a normal day. This changes the design more than
anything else, because **the failure point isn't cooking, it's triggers.**

What follows from it:

- **Two anchored meals, not three.** A five-lunch plan quietly rots in the
  fridge and then the whole system feels like a failure when it was never
  actually attempted. Two meals that reliably happen, plus opportunistic
  protein, matches reality instead of fighting it.
- **The noon burritos are not the problem.** Protein, carbs, warm, fast — after
  that long without eating, that's the brain solving the problem correctly. The
  move isn't "eat earlier" (a willpower fix, and those don't survive). It's:
  when you finally do eat, make the fastest thing in reach a decent thing.
- **Arm's-reach protein, zero assembly.** The day slot needs fully-built food —
  grab, microwave two minutes, eat — plus a visible shelf of no-decision protein
  (shake, cottage cheese, yogurt, jerky, hard-boiled eggs). Dinner is where
  there's energy for components. Different designs for different slots, the same
  way Day C is lighter on the joints than Day A.
- **Anchors beat alarms.** A phone reminder at noon gets swiped away in a
  meeting. What works is stapling food to something that already reliably
  happens: coffee, standup, end of the workday, walking in from the gym. Three
  guaranteed eating triggers a week already exist — Mon/Wed/Fri post-lift.
- **The downstream payoff.** Nothing until noon means genuinely ravenous by
  evening, which is what actually drives the 7pm blowout and the takeout order.
  Fixing the midday anchor tends to fix the evening one without touching it.

## 5. Naming the foods

Foods that go unnamed show up as unplanned deviations and feel like failure.
Named, they're part of the plan. Three categories:

**Non-negotiable pleasures** — scheduled in, not slip-ups. Pizza and beers after
dodgeball are already blessed in the gym plan; the rest should be listed too.

**Load-bearing favourites** — the underrated category. The things that make
decent food actually get eaten: white rice not brown, real cheese on the
vegetables, ranch, hot sauce, bacon in things. If cheese and ranch are what get
a pound of broccoli into you, they're performance-enhancing and they go in the
plan. Swapping them for the "healthier" version produces uneaten Tupperware.

**Depleted-mode fallbacks** — what actually gets eaten when running on empty.
The fallback should be something genuinely liked, not something virtuous that
gets refused at 9pm. If breakfast burritos are the honest answer, the fallback
is a *better burrito*, not a salad.

## 6. What Sean's food list revealed

**Bolay is already the system.** Base, protein, veg, sauce — the exact
four-bucket model, in a format he already voluntarily eats. So the pitch isn't
"try meal prep," it's *Bolay at home*. It also makes Bolay itself a legitimately
on-plan restaurant when nothing's prepped, which is a good thing to have in the
fallback tier.

**His favourites already point at the goal.** Pho is close to ideal for what he
asked for — broth, lean protein, herbs, rice noodles, high volume, low calorie
density — and it's what he'd choose anyway. Ramen is the same shape with more
knobs. There's no version of this that says eat less pho.

**The actual gap:** every single thing on his list is something he *buys*, not
something he *has*. That's the whole problem in one sentence. His taste isn't
the issue — his food supply is entirely external, so when he's depleted at noon
the only path to food he likes runs through an app or a car. The system's job is
getting three or four of those into the fridge.

## 7. The rotation

**A · Asian** — rice, chicken or shrimp, broccoli / edamame / stir-fry veg, and
the sauce does the work: soy-ginger-garlic, sesame, gochujang, or peanut. Four
sauces, one base, four different meals.

**B · Latin** — rice or tortillas, chicken or ground beef, peppers and onions,
chipotle-lime or salsa verde. A burrito bowl — notably, his depleted-mode food
in bowl form.

**C · Tikka masala** — jarred sauce (genuinely good, fifteen minutes), chicken,
rice, naan on the side. Naan stays. Nearly free effort-wise and already on his
list.

**D · Broth bowls** — pho/ramen-style: prepped protein, noodles, good broth,
herbs, a mountain of veg. Highest-volume and most satisfying option, and
probably the one he reaches for most.

**Fallback tier** — better burritos in the freezer, poke-style rice bowls (no
cooking at all), rotisserie chicken + bagged salad + microwave rice, Bolay.

Two prep sessions, two proteins and a base each, sauces rotating. Nothing
removed; more protein and veg by construction.

## 8. On counting calories

Sean asked for fewer calories, and the honest push-back is that tracking is a
high-friction daily habit and usually the first thing to get dropped. **Protein
floor + veg volume + fixed container sizes** gets most of a deficit without a
single number entered. The Tuesday and Saturday games do real work too.

The gym plan already settled the nutrition philosophy — ~150–180g protein a day,
enjoy the rest, don't count calories, check the scale monthly. What it lacked
was an *execution layer*. That's what this is: not a new diet, the "Today's
session" tab for food.

In the app this is `plans.tracking_mode`: `none` (protein and veg floors only —
the default and the point), `protein`, or `full`. An overlay on the same tables,
never a fork.
